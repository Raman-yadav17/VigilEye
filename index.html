It notices the nod before the road does.

VigilEye watches the driver’s face through an ordinary webcam and sounds a warning, spoken and audible, as soon as the eyes stay shut, the head drops or attention leaves the road.

Four signals, one fatigue score

Every driver is calibrated for five seconds, so the thresholds fit their own eyes and posture rather than an average face.

Eyes

Eye aspect ratio from six landmarks per eye. Closure time and the share of time closed over 30 seconds (PERCLOS) drive the score.

Mouth

Mouth aspect ratio. An open mouth held for more than a second counts as a yawn.

Head

A drop of the head against the calibrated baseline, held for a moment, counts as a nod.

Gaze direction

Turning away from the road for 2 seconds warns; 4 seconds is danger. Eye readings are ignored while the head is turned.

From camera frame to alert

Everything runs locally on the driver’s machine. Only danger alerts leave it.

  1. CaptureOpenCV reads the webcam, with optional low-light enhancement.
  2. LandmarksMediaPipe finds 478 face points in every frame.
  3. ScoreEye, mouth and head signals combine into a 0–100 fatigue score.
  4. AlertBeep, spoken warning, event log and a cloud message on danger.

Run it

Python 3.10 or newer, a webcam, and a few packages.

pip install mediapipe opencv-python numpy
python Main.py

Keys: q quit · c recalibrate · l low-light mode · v voice on/off. Spoken warnings work out of the box on Windows.

VigilEye is a driver aid, not a replacement for rest. Pull over when you feel tired.
